Explainer

Which layer is dimming your screen: hardware, software, or neither

Two apps can both say “brightness 40%” and produce completely different pictures. The reason is that brightness is carried by different layers, and only one of them touches the backlight.

The four states

A working implementation has to model this explicitly, because the whole point is the hop from hardware down to software. There are four states, and they are not interchangeable:

  • hardware — the panel's own backlight answers DDC/CI. This is real brightness: the light behind the pixels goes down, and blacks stay black.
  • software — the gamma table is rewritten. The picture gets darker, the backlight does not. At contrast 1 it is a pure scale, so true black stays black. Once contrast drops below 1 it is not: the contrast term pivots around 0.5, which pushes the bottom of the ramp above zero — and that, not the dimming itself, is what turns black into dark grey.
  • unavailable — nothing works. The honest thing is to say so rather than pretend a slider is doing something.
  • unknown — not yet probed. Distinct from unavailable on purpose: one means “Neeson & Sea asked and the panel said no”, the other means “Neeson & Sea has not asked”.

Why “unknown” is not “unavailable”

Collapsing those two into one state is how an app ends up telling you a display is unsupported before it has ever tried. Keeping them apart costs one word in the source and saves a wrong diagnosis.

The delays are part of the design

Fallback is not a single retry. Panels need a moment after wake before they answer DDC/CI at all — replaying the instant the notification lands is exactly why naive wake handlers fail. A real implementation waits 2.5 seconds after wake, and then retries after 1, 2 and 4 seconds.

Why the ladder is kept testable

The retry/fallback ladder is written free of the window toolkit and of any concrete DDC type: the caller injects both the writer and the sleeper. That is what lets the ladder itself be exercised on a machine whose panel ignores DDC/CI entirely — which is the only kind of machine that would otherwise never reach the code.

Honest limits

  • Software dimming is not “the same but cheaper”. It changes the picture, and at low values it visibly lifts the black level.
  • The specific delays are one implementation's choice, measured against its own hardware. They are not a specification, and panels differ.
  • Hardware control is not always better: on a panel that answers DDC/CI slowly, a hybrid can be worse than a clean software dim.
