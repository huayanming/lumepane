Which Mac display app should you use?

There are several good apps for controlling an external display on macOS. This page compares the four you are most likely to find, states what each one does better than ours, and tells you plainly when a different app is the better choice for you. Every fact below is taken from that project's own documentation (checked 19 October 2026).

The one-line answer

  • You want free and open source? Use MonitorControl. It is completely free and does DDC brightness, contrast and volume — plus gamma-table software dimming.
  • You need HiDPI scaling, virtual screens or XDR/HDR extra brightness? Use BetterDisplay. It does far more than display control, and Neeson & Sea does not do those things at all.
  • You want an established brightness app with a free tier? Look at Lunar — free version, or a $23 lifetime Pro licence.
  • You also want safe cleanup, frame-by-frame smooth scrolling, and a one-time purchase with offline activation and no mandatory telemetry? That is what LumePane is for.

When LumePane is the wrong choice

  • You are on macOS 13 or older. LumePane requires macOS 14. The apps above may not.
  • Your display answers DDC/CI and brightness is all you want. A free app does that; paying Neeson & Sea would be wasteful.
  • You need HiDPI or virtual screens. BetterDisplay is built around exactly that; Neeson & Sea is not.
  • You will not use the cleanup, the scrolling or the calibration. Then you are paying for features you do not want.

What LumePane does that the others do not

  • Cleanup with a quarantine. Only 13 kinds of artifacts that have a deterministic rebuild path are touched; everything moves to quarantine for 30 days first; permanent deletion is off by default and requires six conditions to hold.
  • Scrolling that is time-based, not frame-based. 60 Hz and 144 Hz feel the same, and it ships off until you turn it on.
  • Calibration against the curve read when the app first takes over. Repeated adjustment does not accumulate error, and one click hands control back to ColorSync.
  • Offline activation, no device binding. A LP- code is verified on your Mac with an embedded Ed25519 public key — no server call, and the licence is not tied to a device.

A note about the name

If you arrived here looking for Display Pilot: that is a different product. BenQ ships a utility called Display Pilot 2 for its own monitors, and it has no connection to this app. The names are similar; the software is not. This one is LumePane, made by a single developer, and it works with any display macOS can see.
