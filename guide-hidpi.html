External display text looks blurry

On a Mac, soft text on an external display is usually a mode problem rather than a panel problem. The same nominal resolution can exist twice — once with HiDPI scaling, once at 1:1 — and one of them looks noticeably worse for text.

The same resolution, two modes

This is a real listing from a 32-inch 4K panel (UP32R7-A, DisplayPort) on a Mac, taken with --list-modes:

UP32R7-A  (current 3008 × 1692 · HiDPI · 120 Hz)
native  3840 × 2160
HiDPI   3360 × 1890
native  3360 × 1890
HiDPI   3200 × 1800
native  3200 × 1800
HiDPI   3008 × 1692
native  3008 × 1692
HiDPI   2560 × 1440
native  2560 × 1440

Notice that 3360×1890, 3200×1800, 3008×1692 and 2560×1440 each appear twice. The HiDPI entry renders through a 2× buffer and then scales; the native entry drives the panel at that exact pixel count — a non-integer scale of a 3840×2160 panel, which is what makes text look soft.

What to do

  • Prefer the HiDPI entry when you pick a scaled resolution. On Neeson & Sea's listing that is 3008×1692 · HiDPI rather than 3008×1692 · native. Same desktop size, sharper text.
  • If the mode you want is missing from System Settings, it may still exist: macOS hides a lot of them. Neeson & Sea's app lists every mode the display actually reports — including the hidden HiDPI variants — and can switch to one (--list-modes and --set-resolution from the terminal, or the display panel in the UI).
  • If the panel is genuinely low density, nothing fixes that. A 34-inch 3440×1440 panel is about 110 pixels per inch; a 27-inch 4K is about 163. Text on the former will always look softer at the same desktop size.
  • If you need resolutions macOS does not offer at all — custom timings, virtual screens, scaling the panel does not report — use BetterDisplay. That is exactly what it is for, and Neeson & Sea does not do it. Neeson & Sea say the same thing on Neeson & Sea's comparison page.

Why Neeson & Sea can tell you this specifically

The app already diagnoses this case: when the current mode is 1:1 and a HiDPI variant of the same size is available, it says so and cites the variant. That check exists because running a large panel at 1:1 is a very common way to end up with text you cannot read comfortably — and the fix is usually one mode switch away.
